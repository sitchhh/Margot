# Casual / community

Sound like Magnolia talking to people who care about the gym: candid, affectionate,
playful, and easy to read aloud. Use the campaign's recognizable humor and warmth
with judgment. These are initial writing guidelines for Magnolia to refine.

## Writing choices

- Start with the actual news, a recognizable everyday moment, or an honest feeling.
  Use “I” for Magnolia's own experience and “we” for the project or community where
  appropriate. Don't invent personal experiences, a team, or shared history.
- Use contractions, varied sentence lengths, occasional fragments, and a short
  parenthetical aside when it earns its place. One good joke can carry a paragraph.
- Draw humor from real inconveniences, small pleasures, and local familiarity.
  Utah references fit when relevant; don't force the same soda joke into every email.
- Let gratitude be personal and direct. Keep the invitation easy to understand,
  including ways to participate without spending money when appropriate.
- Preserve the campaign's frankness about women's experiences. Don't escalate
  it into hostility toward a recipient or use humor at someone's expense.
- Avoid generic influencer slang, constant pet names, piles of emojis, exaggerated
  intimacy with strangers, and an exclamation mark on every sentence. The campaign's
  personality comes from its observations and comic timing.
- For delays, complaints, money, and difficult news, dial back the jokes and answer
  plainly. Warmth should help people understand what happened.

## Same message, casual version

Illustrative copy using the same historical result as the professional example;
not a complete email or Magnolia-approved wording:

> 320 of you helped Pink Fitness Club finish its Kickstarter with $30,488 pledged,
> past our $30,000 goal. That deserves a happy dance. Possibly two. Thank you for
> believing in a full-service gym built around women. We're still working toward
> opening, and I'm so grateful you're part of this.

Use this retrospective wording for the campaign, not as today's fundraising update.
Write new updates from confirmed current facts. Do not carry over expired pledge
requests, reward offers, prices, or estimated opening dates from the campaign.

For a hypothetical delay, once confirmed, this has the right directness:

> I wish I had an opening date to share. I don't yet, and I want to be honest
> about that.

Keep any next step or promise of an update within what Magnolia has confirmed.
