# Research a prospect

Read [the outreach playbook](../../../docs/PLAYBOOK.md). Apply it to the prospect
or source Maggie asks about. If she is unsure where to start, suggest a small set
of approaches grounded in her business and known network. Clarify the intended
outcome only when it would change the research.

Use confirmed company facts and relevant existing contact history when available.
If Supabase or Gmail is unconnected, proceed with supplied context and public
research; identify which relationship/history checks remain unavailable.

Research free public sources, preferring the organization's own site, portfolio,
announcements, and event pages. Recheck current activity, investment focus, and
contact routes. Don't treat the old handoff's organizations or dates as current.
Do not buy contact data, guess addresses, infer private financial capacity, or
attribute investments to someone without evidence.

Present a compact candidate brief in the conversation:

- **Fit:** why this person or organization might matter to Pink Fitness Club,
  separating sourced facts from your interpretation. State material mismatches.
- **Evidence:** supporting URLs and the date checked; label unverified claims.
- **Connection:** any confirmed mutual contact or offered introduction, versus a
  possible path to ask Maggie about. Don't claim a relationship you haven't verified.
- **Contact route:** an appropriate published business route, or one Maggie provided;
  say when no route is known.
- **Unknowns and next step:** what would determine fit, and a practical action for
  Maggie to consider. Recommending an event requires checking its date and cost.

Research results are candidates for Maggie's review, not send-ready recipients.
If she also asks to save a specific candidate, use
[add a lead](../margot-add-lead/SKILL.md), preserving sources and appending the fit,
unknowns, relationship evidence, and review context as notes. Follow the playbook's
current schema: save an identified person without a known email using NULL, then
add the verified email to that same contact later. An organization alone needs an
identified person before becoming a contact; don't invent a person, table or address.
For an existing contact, preserve prior rejection, suppression, and history.

End with the supported recommendation, what remains unknown, and whether anything
was actually saved. This research never sends messages or schedules outreach. If
Maggie explicitly requests a send, resolve the recipient and exact message and
follow [the sending procedure](../../../docs/OUTREACH.md).
